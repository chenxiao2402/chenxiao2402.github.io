I am **Chen, Xiao (陈骁)**, a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at **Indiana University Bloomington**, advised by **[Prof. Sam Tobin-Hochstadt](https://samth.github.io/)**.

My research in **gradual typing** centers on the following question: when typed and untyped code interact, what guarantees should hold beyond ordinary type soundness, and how can we enforce them at run time? In particular, I study **migratory typing**—the interoperability of typed and untyped modules—through language design, operational semantics, and formalized proofs. I am especially interested in connecting the guarantees a type system promises with the runtime mechanisms that enforce them.

My current work approaches this question from two directions. **[Type as Obligations](#research-types-as-obligations)** studies how values should respect their typing histories and how those histories can guide blame assignment. **[Effect-Typed-Racket](#research-migratory-typing-effects)** explores effect typing and runtime enforcement for algebraic effect handlers in Typed Racket. Looking ahead, I am interested in **substructural types** and the challenges of enforcing resource constraints across typed–untyped boundaries.
