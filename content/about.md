I am **Chen, Xiao (陈骁)**, a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at **Indiana University Bloomington**, advised by **[Prof. Sam Tobin-Hochstadt](https://samth.github.io/)**. My research in **programming languages** focuses on supporting advanced type features in gradually typed languages, where programs are partially typed, without sacrificing soundness. In particular, I am interested in designing language mechanisms and establishing formal guarantees for **migratory typing** — interoperability between typed and untyped modules.

The central question of my research is: **How should the guarantees of advanced type features be formalized in a migratory setting, and how can operational semantics faithfully enforce those guarantees?** My work addresses different dimensions of this question, from defining correctness criteria to designing enforcement mechanisms.

- My **[Type as Obligations](#research-types-as-obligations)** project proposes *Obligation Soundness*, a correctness criterion stronger than type soundness. It formalizes the type annotations associated with a value during execution as its *type obligations*. These obligations capture the complete runtime typing history of each value and, collectively, of an entire evaluation. This leads to the notion of obligation soundness for migratory typing semantics: throughout the evaluation of any valid input program, intermediate values must not violate their type obligations. We also propose a new criterion for blame theorems, using violated obligations as the ground truth for blame assignment.

- My **[Effect-Typed-Racket](#research-migratory-typing-effects)** project retrofits *polymorphic algebraic effect handlers* into **[Typed Racket](https://docs.racket-lang.org/ts-guide/)**, a production-quality typed counterpart of the Racket language, using Racket’s macro system. In addition to developing a formal semantics and establishing its correctness guarantees, we aim to show that the implementation faithfully realizes that semantics. Our approach introduces a second formal model based on Racket primitives, including delimited continuations and runtime contracts. The goal is to prove that the two models agree on the results of evaluating the same valid input, thereby establishing the correctness of the implementation.

- For a future project, I am exploring a framework that automatically generates coordinated resource-enforcement mechanisms for the family of *substructural type systems* across a range of operational semantics for migratory typing. The framework would take two parameters: a substructural specification *R*, which determines resource obligations, and a value-enforcement semantics *S*, which determines ordinary type checks. It would produce an enriched semantics *E(R, S)*. The intended composition theorem would establish that, under suitable compatibility conditions, *E(R, S)* enforces *R* while retaining the appropriate safety guarantee of *S*, with resource violations reported as explicit errors.
